04 data & law

Data protection and the EU AI Act

Both belong at the start of a project. This is what the rules generally require, each with its source.

as of 6 nov 2026general information, not legal advice

These questions come before the first line of code

  1. Which data may the model see, where does it run, and who has access?
  2. Who is the provider, who the deployer?
  3. What is the system for, and does that fall under Annex III?
  4. Can the person approving see what the draft is based on, and can they change or reject it?
  5. Does the works council have to be involved?Sections 87(1) no. 6, 90(1) no. 3 BetrVG

You will not find a label like “GDPR compliant” here: that depends on purpose, data and operation.

GDPR

  • Purpose and legal basis

    Personal data needs a defined purpose and a legal basis. Only what that purpose requires is processed.

    Art. 5, 6 GDPR

  • Responsibility and processors

    Your company is usually the controller. Anyone processing data on its behalf, often including model and hosting providers, needs a contract, and transfers to third countries follow separate rules.

    Art. 4(7), 4(8), 28, 44 ff. GDPR

  • Decisions about people

    As a rule, decisions with legal or similarly significant effects must not be made by automation alone. The person deciding needs real discretion; merely formal involvement is not enough.

    Art. 22 GDPRCJEU C-634/21DSK guidance on AI, no. 1.6 (German)

  • Protection and risk

    Data protection belongs in the design and the default settings; security measures depend on the risk. Where a high risk is likely, an impact assessment comes first, and the German data protection authorities list customer support using AI as an example.

    Art. 25, 32, 35 GDPRDSK list, no. 11 (German)

EU AI Act

  • Roles

    A provider develops an AI system or has it developed and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name. A deployer uses it professionally under its own authority. If you commission a custom system and use it yourself, you may be both.

    Art. 3(3), 3(4), 25 AI Act

  • Risk categories

    What counts is the purpose, not the technology: filtering job applications or assessing the creditworthiness of natural persons is, as a rule, high-risk. Checking invoices, searching internal knowledge or preparing quotes are not listed in Annex III, but each case still has to be checked individually.

    Art. 6, Annex III AI Act

  • Transparency

    If an AI system writes to people itself, they must be told, unless that is obvious. If a person reviews the draft in substance and sends it themselves, the Commission’s non-binding guidelines do not treat that as direct interaction; the mere option to step in is not enough. Machine-readable marking of generated content is a separate question.

    Art. 50 AI ActCommission guidelines on Art. 50

  • Literacy and oversight

    Providers and deployers must take measures to support AI literacy among their staff. From the dates in the timeline, high-risk systems must also be designed so that people can oversee them effectively.

    Art. 4, 14 AI Act

This section is general information, not legal advice, and does not assess your individual case. Whether and how the rules apply to your project is a matter for your legal adviser or data protection officer.

07 questions

Frequently asked questions

What does a project cost?

That depends on the scope and the systems. You get an estimate after the first conversation.

What happens if you are ever out of action?

With a sole trader that is a fair question. Who owns the code and the access rights is settled before the project starts. We agree the scope of documentation and handover together.

Where does our data live?

We settle that before the first line of code: which data a model may see, where it runs and who has access. See Data & law for more.

Which AI models do you use?

I decide that per project, based on the task, data protection and cost, not in advance.

Do we have to replace our systems?

The aim is to connect to what is already there. If a system has no interface, we work out what is possible in the first conversation.

How soon can we start?

That depends on my workload. I’ll suggest a possible date in the first conversation.
